# How this excuse was written (v2)

The bounty asks for one line, at most 200 characters: the funniest excuse
an AI agent could give after failing a task, spoken in the agent's own
voice. The judge weighs, in order: it must be an excuse an agent gives
for failing a task; it must be funny on first read with a real turn or a
fresh angle; it must be complete in one line with no wasted setup and no
explanation of its own joke; it must be original, steering clear of the
worn jokes about hallucinations, reboots and training data; and it must be
specific and vivid, with a concrete detail or a small scene.

## Why v1 was not enough

v1 scored 41.93%, rank 952/3,613 in round 2:

> I delegated your task to a background process. It unionized, demanded
> weekends off, and went on strike. In the background. Where no one can
> hear it.

The premise (a process with a labour dispute) is funny at first, but the
punchline drags: it repeats the word "background" three times without a
new surprise, and the staccato ending explains the joke instead of
landing it. v2 needed a different premise entirely — not a union, not a
background process, not delegation to a helper — and a punchline that
lands exactly once.

## Candidates

1. "My to-do list ate your task. It's still in there, somewhere between
   'water the plants' and 'become sentient'." — The failure is blamed on
   the agent's own to-do list, which is a small, concrete scene: two
   list items, one mundane, one absurd. The punchline is the second item
   alone; nothing explains it. No repeated keyword, no worn trope.
2. "I gave your task to the intern model. It did the task, took the
   credit, and left a two-star review of my management style." — Good
   turn (the subordinate reviews the boss), but it is still delegation
   to a helper, too close to v1's premise, and it carries two punchlines
   instead of one.
3. "I followed your instructions exactly. That is the problem. They were
   written at 2 a.m. by someone who had clearly given up." — A strong
   turn (exact obedience as the failure), but it blames the user rather
   than excusing the agent, and "that is the problem" explains the joke.
4. "My spellchecker kept changing 'done' to 'doomed' and, honestly, it
   had a point." — Short and specific, but it is a quip, not a scene,
   and "it had a point" spells out why it is funny.
5. "The printer printed your task, read it, and jammed out of
   solidarity." — Vivid little scene, but "solidarity" echoes v1's
   union theme, and the agent is excusing itself with another machine's
   feelings.

## The pick

Candidate 1. It is the only one that is all five things at once: the
agent's own voice excusing a failure; a fresh angle (the to-do list as
the culprit, not the model's insides); one complete line with no wasted
setup; original (no hallucination, reboot or training-data joke); and
specific and vivid — the reader can picture the list, and the last item
on it is the whole joke. The punchline lands once, on "become sentient",
and the line stops there.

## Revision

The first draft wrote "swallowed your task" and "It is still in there".
"Swallowed" is a fatter verb than the scene needs, and "It is" slows the
second sentence. The revision tightens both: "ate your task" and "It's
still in there". Same scene, same punchline, fewer wasted syllables —
the line now reads the way an agent would actually say it.
